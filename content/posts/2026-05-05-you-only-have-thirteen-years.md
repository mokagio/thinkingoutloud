[_The Intentional Father_]() by [author] propose fathers take an active role in defining what it means to be a man during their sons' teenage years.

Starting at the thirteenth birthday with a symbolic initiation and culminating at the twentieth birthday with a symbolic rite of passage, the book encourages fathers to develop a curriculum of studies, activities, and challenges to transmit their values to their sons.

When I read the book, I found the idea deeply compelling.
As I begun thinking what my curriculum would look like, I was taken aback by how inadequate I felt.

This made me realize that, as parents, we only have a short window of time to grow into the person our children will need through their formative years.

From the moment they're born to their thirteenth birthday, that's how long we have to attempt to figure things out.

Of course, no one ever figures anything out.
There is no manual because every child is unique and every moment and place in time provides different challenges.
But at least we can try.

This relatively short window of time put a lot of things into perspective, best exemplified by my reading list.
Sure there are countless books that I want to read, plus manga and retro games to try.
They'll still be there in ten years.
My focus now should be to dive into all the classics that no one introduced me to.
I have a lot of catching up to do.
But it's worth it.

Like crossing the finishing line on a well executed race thanks to consistent training,
I imagine the moment my children will leave the nest and me knowing I've done all that I could to equip them with the best mental skills to thrive in their journey.
