David Epstein's [_Range_](https://www.amazon.com/Range-Generalists-Triumph-Specialized-World/dp/0735214506) is a book I often recommend because of the intriguing case it makes for the value of breadth and exploration in a world that so often encourages early specialization.
So, when interviews with David appeared in my podcast feed as part of the promotion for his new book on the value of constraints [_Inside the Box_](https://www.amazon.com/Inside-Box-Constraints-Make-Better/dp/0593715713), I was eager for new paradigm-shifting ideas.

Alas, I already knew many of the stories, studies, and strategies he recommended.

That's not to discredit to David's work, I'm still curious to read the book and bet I'll enjoy it, but it reminded me that, when it comes to productivity, I keep coming across the same handful of core ideas.

Productivity needs practice more than it needs reading.

After you've read five or six _good_ books, from authors such as Cal Newport, Oliver Burkeman, Tom DeMarco, David Allen, and of course David Epstein, you'll have encountered most of the core concepts and strategies.
More importantly, you'll have plenty of things to try yourself.
Pick a few, stick with them for a couple of months, and see what happens.

You can't read your way into better results.
At some point, you have to actually work.
