title: 'Double Dipping Training'

Since leaning more into AI to write and review code, I've been growing more concerned with losing cognitive fitness.

Delegating physical labor to machines means our muscles don't exercise enough and become weak.
Could delegating cognition to AI have a similar weakening effect on our brains?

I don't know how robust the analogy between physical and cognitive fitness is, but there is enough anecdotal evidence to make the concepts of strength and stamina applicable, if not physiologically, at least as framing devices, to our ability to focus.
Mental stamina is how long you can stay on task without getting distracted.
Cognitive strength is the depth of your concentration, how many moving pieces can you keep in your mind, how many moves ahead can you think.

As AI pervades more of our jobs, it might be time to develop [training plans](https://www.giolodi.com/p/a-training-plan-for-cognitive-fitness) for our minds as well as our bodies.

Here's a simple way to train the body while also growing mental stamina: **Exercise without headphones.**

We have tech that allows us to bring our favorite music, podcasts, and audiobooks with us on any activity, be it on the road, in the gym, or even under water.
Entertainment can make working out more palatable, _boredom_ might also be a crucial part of the training.

By intentionally exposing ourselves to boredom, we remind our brains that it's okay not to be stimulated all the time.
So, next time something at work doesn't deliver immediate feedback—or the AI spends more time "thinking"—we won't switch to another task and leave the current one unfinished.
More importantly, as problems become harder and demand focused time, we won't sabotage our chances of success by giving up for something easier.
