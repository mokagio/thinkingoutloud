There's [an old conversation](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/ep-31-ryan-holiday-on-ranch-life-fostering-discipline/id1515786216?i=1000492880251) between Cal Newport and Ryan Holiday where they comment on their prolific output as writers.
They agree that a crucial factor is **constantly interacting with ideas**.

Reading is the entry point, but it's not enough.
You need to pluck ideas from the input stream and reflect on them.

Practical example: Read, highlight passages, extract them in digital notes, add your commentary.

Another: Go for a walk with an audiobook, pause when you run into something that makes you think, reflect on it for the remainder of the walk, write down your conclusion once back home.

I find engaging with ideas this way to be far more nourishing than reading or listening only.

You don't need to share your notes.
They can remain confined to the privacy of your notebook.
Having said that, the best way to discover errors in your takes is to subject them to real world criticism.

Once you objectified your views by writing them down, publishing them, even in as simple a format as this plain blog, gives you a way to share them with friends for criticism.

Regardless of whether you share your thoughts or keep them to yourself, I encourage to you go beyond consumption and engage in [studying and savoring](https://www.giolodi.com/p/stop-consuming-start-savoring) instead.
