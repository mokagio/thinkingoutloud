I love AI.
I hate AI[<sup id="footnote-id">*</sup>](#fn1).

I love what the tech does.
I hate what its makers do to sell it.

I love how it lowers the barrier to entry.
I hate how it lowers the quality bar.

I love how it works for me at any hour.
I hate how it can never get past good enough.

I love how it answers my all of my questions.
I have how it has now idea what it's talking about.

I love the excitement of seeing new tech come online.
I hate the uncertainty of whether any of this is going to last.

Here are some questions I don't have the answer to.

As a software engineer, can you afford _not_ to use AI?
Leave aside the fact that it would be career suicide in many enterprises.
Think only of "productivity".
Would going back to a time before autocompletion on steroids be better?

I think not.
Yet, directing agents is nowhere near as satisfying as coding yourself.
It might be more productive (at least in the short term) but it's definitely not as nourishing.

Writing elegant code, drawing a beautiful portrait, composing a moving melody—those are activities you can be proud of.
(I put that em dash there, by the way, not an LLM.)
Can you say the same thing of code, images, or music that a model produced for you?
_Can you be proud of a prompt?_

I wonder how much of this is merely a transition problem, a growing pain.
Did carpenters who were proud of the straightness of their hand saw cuts ever feel that adopting power saws robbed them of their craft?
Maybe there are stories and debates about this topic in some archive, one just has to find them.

Maybe the coders of tomorrow will be like today's carpenters.
Still skilled, still in control, simply using more powerful tools that amplify their ability.

But does AI-assisted coding actually improve coders' ability?
A power saw is legitimately faster and better than a hand saw.
Can we say the same for LLM-generated code?
Sure you can get AI to regurgitate thousands of lines of code, but how good is that code?

Does adopting AI atrophies our minds like moving from hand to power saw atrophies our muscles?
Weak muscles can be strengthened in the gym.
Can we do the same for our minds?

From the moment I was allowed a calculator in high school, my mental math skills began to decline.
That's an acceptable trade, as the ability to do large sums in one's head rarely affects everyday life.
Can the same be said for cognition in general?
Will delegating more and more of our knowledge work to AI erode our ability to think, like social media has been eroding our attention?
Can a civilization that offloads thinking to a machine that replies based on statistic ever create something new?
Or is it doomed to stagnation?

Here I am, worrying about a plateau in human cognition and a hard cap on the quality of programming languages...
Are these legitimate concerns, or are they just the product of hype?

Maybe, despite my aversion for AI doomers and frontier labs' PR departments alike, I'm caught in the worldview they are selling.
Loud people with vested interest are trying to convince us that AI is the end—of the world, of our employment, of poverty, take your pick—and even a critic can't escape that framing.

I feel like I'm in a roller coaster cart riding the Gartner hype cycle, on the edge between the _peak of inflated expectations_ and the massive fall into the _trough of disillusionment_.

Maybe all this navel gazing is for naught.
[Uber's COO questioning the cost of AI tokenmaxxing](https://www.businessinsider.com/uber-coo-andrew-macdonald-ai-token-spending-harder-justify-2026-5) and [NVIDIA's VP saying AI costs 'far' more than his employees](https://sg.finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-vp-says-ai-costs-180821730.html) might be the heralds of the bubble bursting.
If it wasn't for the tragic downstream economic consequences, I'd be rooting for it.
AI wouldn't go away, but at least we'd have some sanity and focus back in the discourse.

I have more concerns to vent and a few practical ideas to explore, but I think that's enough for the moment.

Leave a comment below if you have any resolution for my contradictions or answers to my conundrums.
