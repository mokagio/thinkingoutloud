title: 'More Wittgensteinian Than Wittgenstein'
---

[_Wittgenstein's Poker_](https://www.amazon.com/Wittgensteins-Poker-Ten-Minute-Argument-Philosophers/dp/0060936649) by David Edmonds and John Eidinow is a charming investigation into the competing philosophies of Ludwig Wittgenstein and Karl Popper, the Austrian culture in which they developed, and the impact they had on the intellectual discourse.

In their research, the authors observe how charismatic Wittgenstein was and how this affected the people around him, especially his students.

> The philosopher Gilbert Ryle noted that on his occasional visits to the club [the Moral Science Club] "veneration for Wittgenstein was so incontinent that my mentions of any other philosopher were greeted with jeers."

Apparently, it was impossible for students not to absorb some of Wittgenstein's mannerisms and exclamations.
Some went as far as dressing in the same style as their master.

> "[Wittgenstein was] surrounded with acolytes in tweed jackets and white open-necked shirts identical to his own," and the authors conclude: "Wittgenstein attracted disciples rather than students."

The mimicking did not stop at the wardrobe, however.

> How religiously the inner circle of the disciples followed the master has a comical air to it:
> sleeping in narrow beds, wearing sneakers, carrying vegetables in string bags to let them breathe, and putting celery in water when serving it for dinner.
> But this was not just a matter of amusing idiosyncrasies being aped:
> his students were also liable to change their attitude toward life in general, adopting Wittgenstein's "proud and even contemptuous austerity," learning to scorn what they had previously enjoyed as harmless luxuries as "utterly trivial and unworthy of attachment."
> In fact they could be more Wittgensteinian than Wittgenstein, for the master himself was not as ascetic as is often presented.

Those who think for themselves, whether creative geniuses like Wittgenstein or average Joes who built their worldview on real-life experience, know when to do and when not to do.
But imitators do not think for themselves.
They adopt a prepackaged set of behaviors wholesale, without any of the work that led to developing them.

Lacking the intellectual foundation to iterate on what they adopted, they can only compete on _who is the best at copying_.
The result: The disciples become more radical and rigid than the master.
