title: 'The Bigger the Platform…'
---

If there’s something that the study of history shows, it is that despite all our advances, whether technological or moral, human nature remains pretty much the same.

Take this passage from Vannevar Bush’s biography [_Endless Frontier_](https://www.amazon.com/Endless-Frontier-Vannevar-Engineer-American/dp/1501196456):

> “The sudden prominence which has been given to physicists due to war progress in applications of physics has gone to the heads of quite a few people,” Bush wrote an MIT professor.
> “They proceed to testify on subjects that they have met only for the first time and recently, which is all right, but they do so with a didacticism which is all wrong.
> If they do not look out they will end up by becoming discredited.”

Bush was referring to the physicists involved in World War II, but the remark could be applied today to a vast range of practitioners who, once in the spotlight, go on to opine _with didacticism_ on a variety of topics outside their field of expertise.

How many people who have spent a lifetime mastering a subject forget that the firm handle they have on it does not apply across the board?
How rare is it to hear experts in one field [refrain from answering](https://www.giolodi.com/p/strange-truths-notes-and-highlights) questions on other fields?

Once again, I'm reminded of [Feynman's first principle](https://www.giolodi.com/p/richard-feynmans-call-for-intellectual):
_you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool_.

Let's stay humble.
Hard work and concrete results might give us a platform, but the bigger the platform, the bigger the fool we can make of ourselves.
