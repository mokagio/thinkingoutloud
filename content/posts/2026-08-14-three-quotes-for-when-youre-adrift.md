title: "Three Quotes for When You're Adrift"

Have you ever felt like a boat that is in perfect working order, yet adrift at the sea?
There's nothing _wrong_ with you, yet something is clearly missing.

I've been feeling a bit like that for a while.
When I'm able to step outside my own head, I can see that everything is fine.
I have nothing to complain about and plenty to be grateful for.
And yet...

I'll often catch myself ruminating, stewing in this broth of unease.
One way out, when I'm self-aware enough, is to switch from rumination to pondering.

These are three passages I've been pondering to find some wind for my sails.

From David Deutsch's [_The Beginning of Infinity_](https://www.amazon.com/Beginning-Infinity-Explanations-Transform-World/dp/0143121359):

> Happiness is a state of continually solving one’s problems, they conjecture.
> Unhappiness is caused by being chronically baulked in one’s attempts to do that.
> And solving problems itself depends on knowing how; so, external factors aside, unhappiness is caused by not knowing how.

From James Carse's [_Finite and Infinite Games_](https://www.amazon.com/Finite-Infinite-Games-James-Carse/dp/1476731713):

> An infinite player does not begin working for the purpose of filling up a period of time with work, but for the purpose of filling work with time.
> Work is not an infinite player’s way of passing time, but of engendering possibility.
> Work is not a way of arriving at a desired present and securing it against an unpredictable future, but of moving toward a future which itself has a future.

From Viktor Frankl's [_Man's Search for Meaning_](https://www.amazon.com/Mans-Search-Meaning-Viktor-Frankl/dp/080701429X):

> Ultimately, man should not ask what the meaning of his life is, but rather must recognize that it is he who is asked.
> In a word, each man is questioned by life; and he can only answer to life by answering for his own life; to life he can only respond by being responsible.

Happy sailing.
